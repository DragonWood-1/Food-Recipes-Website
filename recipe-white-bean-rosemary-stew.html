Slow Cooker White Bean & Rosemary Stew

Original recipe · Serves 6 · 10 minutes prep + slow cook

Dried beans were made for the slow cooker. Given a long, gentle simmer they turn silky and creamy in a way canned beans never quite match, and they throw off a savory broth as they go. This vegetarian stew leans on a Parmesan rind and a handful of rosemary for depth, then wakes up at the end with a bright lemon-garlic gremolata.

⏱️ 10 min prep 🍲 7–8 hrs on low 🍽️ Serves 6 🥦 Vegetarian

Ingredients

  • For the stew
  • 1 lb dried cannellini or great northern beans (no need to pre-soak)
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 2 celery stalks, diced
  • 6 cloves garlic, smashed
  • 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 Parmesan rind (optional but worth it)
  • 8 cups vegetable stock
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1.5 tsp salt, plus more to taste
  • 3 cups chopped kale or chard, stems removed
  • Lemon-garlic gremolata
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 1 small clove garlic, finely grated
  • 1/4 cup parsley, finely chopped

Instructions

  1. Load the slow cooker. Add the beans, onion, carrots, celery, garlic, rosemary, bay leaves, Parmesan rind, olive oil, and stock. Stir once. Do not add salt yet — adding it now can keep the beans from softening.
  2. Cook low and slow. Cover and cook on LOW for 7–8 hours, until the beans are completely tender and creamy. (On HIGH, check at around 4 hours, but low gives the best texture.)
  3. Season and thicken. Remove the rosemary stems, bay leaves, and Parmesan rind. Stir in the salt. For a creamier stew, scoop out about a cup of beans, mash them, and stir them back in — an easy way to thicken without flour or cream.
  4. Add the greens. Stir in the kale or chard and cook another 10–15 minutes with the lid on, until wilted and tender. Taste and adjust salt; beans need more than you'd expect.
  5. Make the gremolata. Combine the lemon zest, grated garlic, and parsley. Ladle the stew into bowls and finish each with a generous spoonful, plus a drizzle of olive oil.

The salt rule for dried beans

Salt early and beans can stay stubbornly firm; salt at the end and they cook creamy, then drink up seasoning beautifully. The same goes for acidic ingredients like tomatoes — add them after the beans are tender, not before.

Make it vegan

Skip the Parmesan rind and add 1 tablespoon of white miso along with the greens for a similar savory depth. A teaspoon of nutritional yeast stirred in at the end reinforces that cheesy note.

Serving & storage

This is a meal in a bowl with crusty bread, but it's also excellent spooned over polenta or toast rubbed with garlic. It keeps 4 days in the fridge and freezes well for up to 3 months — the flavor only deepens.

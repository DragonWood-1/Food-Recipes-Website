Crispy Brown Butter & Sage Gnocchi

Original recipe · Serves 3–4 · About 20 minutes

Here's a trick that feels like cheating: skip boiling the gnocchi entirely and pan-fry the shelf-stable kind straight from the package. They turn golden and crisp outside, stay pillowy inside, and soak up a nutty brown butter with crackling fried sage. Five main ingredients, and it tastes far fancier than the effort it takes.

⏱️ 20 minutes 🍽️ Serves 3–4 📊 Easy 🥦 Vegetarian

Ingredients

  • 1 lb shelf-stable potato gnocchi (not refrigerated/fresh)
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 15–20 fresh sage leaves
  • 2 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan, plus more to serve
  • Zest of 1/2 lemon
  • Salt, pepper, and a pinch of chili flakes

Instructions

  1. Crisp the gnocchi. Heat the olive oil and 1 tbsp of the butter in a large nonstick or cast-iron skillet over medium-high. Add the gnocchi in a single layer (don't crowd — work in batches if needed) and cook 5–6 minutes, tossing occasionally, until golden and crisp on several sides. Move to a plate.
  2. Fry the sage. Lower the heat to medium. Add the remaining 3 tbsp butter. Once melted, add the sage leaves and fry until they go crisp and the butter starts to smell nutty, about 2 minutes. Scoop the leaves onto a paper towel.
  3. Brown the butter. Keep cooking the butter, swirling, until it turns amber and smells toasty — watch closely, it goes from brown to burnt fast. Add the garlic and chili flakes and cook 30 seconds.
  4. Toss and finish. Return the gnocchi to the pan and toss to coat in the brown butter. Off the heat, add the Parmesan and lemon zest and toss until glossy. Season with salt and pepper.
  5. Serve. Plate it up, crumble the fried sage over the top, and finish with extra Parmesan. Eat right away, while it's crisp.

How to brown butter without burning it

Use a light-colored pan if you can, so you can see the color. The butter will foam, quiet down, then suddenly the milk solids at the bottom turn golden-brown and smell like toasted nuts. That's your cue — pull it from the heat immediately, because residual heat keeps it cooking.

Make it a fuller meal

  • Add greens: a few handfuls of baby spinach or chopped kale wilted in at the end.
  • Add protein: white beans tossed in with the gnocchi, or crisped pancetta for non-vegetarians.
  • Add sweetness: roasted butternut squash cubes are a classic match for brown butter and sage.

A note on the gnocchi

The shelf-stable, vacuum-packed gnocchi found in the pasta aisle crisps up best for this method. Fresh or frozen gnocchi hold more moisture and are better boiled, then briefly crisped. Either way, don't skip the single layer in the pan — that's how you get the crust.
